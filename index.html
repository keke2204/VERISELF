01 / THE THESIS THE ERASURE OF THE CONSENT BOUNDARY

Why the human likeness demands cryptographic armor.

CRITICAL PREMISE

When an image leaves the local camera roll, it ceases to exist merely as visual representation; it becomes unquantified training data for automated identity commodification.

The rapid maturation of latent diffusion architectures and automated scrapers has eliminated the historical cost of digital surveillance. Where visual identification once required targeted human observation, autonomous spiders now index faces at billions per day, extracting high-dimensional coordinate embeddings that outlive the original context in which the photograph was captured.

Generative adversarial models and fine-tuning pipelines (LoRA, DreamBooth, FaceSwap) require as few as two unperturbed high-resolution portraits to reconstitute a subject's facial topography into synthetic non-consensual depictions. This constitutes a fundamental asymmetry: the individual cannot retreat from the public sphere, yet participating in visual communication forfeits their biometric sovereignty.

VeriSelf intervenes directly at the raster level. Rather than appealing to regulatory restraint or post-hoc legislative remedies, the system operationalizes mathematical obfuscation before any packet reaches a remote server.

By calculating the spatial gradient of the image tensor and injecting localized noise patterns into bands of maximal visual complexity, the human observer perceives an unaltered portrait with complete tonal depth. Simultaneously, convolutional neural networks and transformer-based vision backbones encounter corrupted landmark vectors that fail to converge, neutralizing downstream feature indexation.
